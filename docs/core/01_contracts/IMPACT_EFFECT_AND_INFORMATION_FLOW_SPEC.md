# UET Impact, Effect, and Information-Flow Specification

## Status

`CANDIDATE_ONTOLOGY_REFINEMENT / PHYSICAL_CARRIER_AND_UNIT_MAPPING_OPEN`

This specification records the distinction between `impact` and `effect` that
was previously being used interchangeably in the UET discussions.

## The distinction

### Impact

An impact is a physical interaction in which the source and receiving systems
are coupled through a force, field interaction, stress-energy exchange, mass
transfer, momentum transfer, or another declared physical coupling:

\[
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{impact}}:
(X_A,X_B)
\longrightarrow
(X'_A,X'_B).
\]

The impact changes a physical state or its conserved quantities. In a declared
lane it must have a physical carrier, units, conservation law, and source term.

Examples include collision, gravitational scattering, electromagnetic force,
annihilation, absorption, emission, and a detector interaction.

### Effect

An effect is the influence or input-output relation that a receiving system
constructs from a signal, trace, or interaction:

\[
\mathcal E_{A\rightarrow B}:
(\Psi_{\mathrm{carrier}},D_B)
\longrightarrow
Y_B.
\]

The effect is not defined by mass transfer. It is defined by a change in the
receiver's state, record, inference, or subsequent behaviour that depends on
the incoming signal. The source's mass does not have to travel to the receiver
for an effect to occur.

An effect is therefore a relational/measurement layer, not an additional
substance. If the receiver's physical state actually changes, the detector
interaction and the carrier's energy-momentum still belong to the physical
layer. “Information only” means that the model is describing the payload or
input-output relation, not denying the physical carrier.

## Causal sequence

The full sequence is

```text
source matter/field behaviour
  -> physical impact or transition
  -> generated trace R_gen
  -> carrier Psi_carrier
  -> detector/receiver interaction
  -> effect E_A->B
  -> observer record R_obs or Y_B
  -> possible new receiver behaviour
```

The carrier may be a photon, neutrino, gravitational-wave perturbation, field
excitation, or another declared physical channel. The carrier identity is not
fixed by the word `effect`.

## Relation to mass and information

The distinction can be summarized as:

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
\text{impact} &:& \text{physical coupling and state/energy-momentum change},\\
\text{carrier} &:& \text{physical excitation that propagates the influence},\\
\text{effect} &:& \text{receiver-side input-output consequence},\\
\text{information} &:& \text{state/correlation calculated from that relation}.
\end{array}
\]

Thus:

- a photon can carry an effect without carrying rest mass;
- a neutrino can carry information from a weak interaction while having a
  nonzero mass eigenstate in the standard model;
- a positron can participate in an impact or phase-changing reaction, but is not
  thereby the universal information carrier;
- the same physical carrier can produce different records for different
  observers or detectors.

## Relation to the UET variables

The current UET interpretation is:

\[
\text{impact history}
\rightarrow
B_{\mathrm{sys}}
\rightarrow C
\rightarrow \Phi,\Pi
\rightarrow R_{\mathrm{gen}}
\rightarrow \Psi_{\mathrm{carrier}}
\rightarrow R_{\mathrm{obs}}^{(O)}.
\]

`C` is the collective behaviour coordinate. `Phi` is a candidate physical
response. `R_gen` is the trace generated by completed physical behaviour.
`R_obs` is the received or reconstructed record. None of these symbols is a
universal identity for mass, photon, positron, neutrino, or information.

The current `I_trace` implementation is closest to `R_gen`. It does not yet
derive a photon, positron, or neutrino carrier from the UET equation.

## Phase-change interpretation

The user's phase-change hypothesis can be represented without prematurely
choosing a particle:

\[
X_{\mathrm{mass\text{-}bearing}}^{(a)}
\xrightarrow{\text{impact, energy exchange, or instability}}
X^{(b)}
\xrightarrow{\text{carrier channel}}
\Psi_{\mathrm{carrier}}.
\]

In a particular standard-physics lane, the final state may contain photons,
massive particles, neutrinos, or several products. The original organized
mass-bearing identity can end while energy-momentum and the causal trace
continue in another form. This is a candidate “termination of a phase,” not a
claim that every fast object automatically becomes light.

## Collective balance

The proposed UET meaning of equilibrium is not absence of behaviour. It is a
configuration in which interacting behaviours remain mutually compatible over
the declared timescale:

\[
\text{dynamic compatibility}
\Rightarrow
\text{bounded collective behaviour}
\Rightarrow
\text{longer persistence of the system pattern}.
\]

For an orbital system, the Newtonian/relativistic many-body equations remain the
standard baseline. UET may test whether a collective coordinate `C` measures a
coherence or persistence property of that baseline, but it has not yet derived
an independent orbital law.

## Prohibited conflations

Do not write:

- `effect = no physical carrier`;
- `information = a new substance`;
- `impact = only observer interpretation`;
- `positron = the opposite energy that cancels an electron`;
- `massless = physically absent`;
- `R_gen = R_obs`;
- `C = mass` without a lane-specific map.
