Current curved 3+1 boundary research

Recent Core work has closed several source-matched frozen-coefficient boundary-symbol lanes, without closing the full curved 3+1 parent:

  • Zero normal shift: Rinne Fourier-Laplace/Kreiss symbol.
  • Positive normal shift: sampled positive-shift symbol lane; closure is limited to the preregistered shift/frequency samples.
  • Negative normal shift: sampled symbol including the degenerate case and no-go for one uniform Kreiss constant over the full negative-shift family. The latter does not rule out a finite constant for each fixed shift.
  • Exact negative-shift degeneracy: Jordan normalization audit derives an ODE-consistent chain and nonzero determinant at zeta=-beta for -1<beta<0. The printed source normalization/determinant mismatch remains flagged; this does not close a complex-frequency inverse bound. See Rinne 2006, Section 3.2.
  • Low-frequency positive-real transfer: finite uniform transfer bound, which is mathematically finite but too loose to be a useful stability estimate.
  • Source/state norm interface: norm-coordinate audit maps the 30 source channels and 40 state components explicitly. Independent-component Euclidean and full-tensor Frobenius conventions differ by at most sqrt(2), so the prior lane bound translates to <sqrt(2)*1e67 without making it practically useful.
  • Tangential finite-grid comparison: LGL resolution refinement and weak-SAT control. The growing modes belong to the tested differential-target discretization; the stable incoming-value SAT is a different boundary condition, so neither result alone decides continuum Rinne stability.
  • Normal incidence: the exact slab-cycle audit closes non-amplification for its restricted linear wave lane only.

Still open at the parent level: full first-order GH constraint-preserving boundary evolution, tangential/corner treatment, and dimensional-observable mapping.
